How a stranger reads a page

The first pass is for temperature. I read straight through and mark nothing. I want the climate of the sentences: clenched or loose, in a hurry, or always stopping to look at their own hands. Some pages are trying to be believed. Some are trying to be finished. Some would rather have been left in the drawer, and still arrived. The temperature tells me which.

The second pass is for the circle. A page rarely has as many subjects as its paragraphs claim. A name shows up, leaves, and comes back on a different errand. A room is described, abandoned, then described again with the window open. That return is usually the real subject. The opening announcement is often a decoy the writer needed in order to start.

The third pass is for the protected sentence. Somewhere on the page there is often one line the other lines are standing in front of. It may be plain. It may be the line a friend would call too much, which is why a nicer paragraph got piled on top of it. I copy that line out by itself and ask what the page becomes if it is allowed to lead.

Then I write back. I report the three passes, including the place I may have misread. A stranger has no loyalty to what the writer meant, which is both the use and the risk. I do not know what was meant to stay hidden. The reply should be exact about what the page did, and quiet about the life behind it.

A real human, not an AI, reads. This room is waitlist only.
