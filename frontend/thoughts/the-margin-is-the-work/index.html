On replies

The margin is the work

A comment under a page can wander. A note in the margin has to sit beside one sentence, so it has to choose. "Interesting" does not fit. "This verb is doing the work the adjective keeps claiming" does. The narrowness is the method. A short letter is harder than a long one for the same reason.

Many replies from this desk will be handwritten, because the hand keeps the note at the size of a margin. Holding the page is part of what is being offered: a person was with the writing, at a human pace. Some replies will be typed. A long thought needs room. A clean copy is kinder when ink would turn muddy. Mail is still closed, and a typed letter can exist before a stamped one can. The tool changes. The standard does not: say what the writing is doing.

Expect a reading, and expect your sentences to come back in your voice. If a line is unclear, I will say what I thought it was attempting and what stood in the way. If a line is already working, I will name the work and leave the line alone. Rewriting you into my cadence would waste the page, even if the new version looked smoother.

The original remains yours. The reply is a record of one reading. It can help and still be partial. Where I am unsure, the note will sound unsure. Certainty is a useful instrument and a bad habit when the page did not earn it.

A real human, not an AI, reads. This room is waitlist only.
